# Jen Dva Tri

A digital adaptation of a folk game played with 1:64 model cars on chalk-drawn
tracks outdoors. Turn-based finger-push racing — precision, not speed.

## Language

**Car**:
A 1:64 scale model car, plastic, metal, or mixed. Cars differ in wheel size
and suspension feel, making each model handle differently.
_Avoid_: vehicle, racer

**Push**:
A brief strike against the Car with the side of a bent index finger — riding
the Car is illegal, but a strike lasting a short bounded window is legal,
and its direction may change within that window (curve Push). A Push can hit
any side of the Car in any direction.
_Avoid_: flick, tap, ride

**Turn**:
One player's sequence of three Pushes, taken in immediate succession.

**Track**:
A corridor drawn in chalk between two Boundary lines on a hard surface
(concrete). Shapes: A-to-B, loop (A-to-A), or multiple loops.

**Boundary**:
One of the two chalk lines delimiting the Track.

**Checkpoint**:
A thin chalk line across the Track, just like the Starting Line. A resetting
Car returns to the closest Checkpoint behind it.
_Avoid_: save point, marker

**Reset**:
Placing a Car behind a Checkpoint line — anywhere along it, in any
orientation the owner chooses (e.g. angled for a curve ahead).

**Out of Track**:
Three or more wheels fully beyond a Boundary. A wheel counts as inside the
Track while any part of it touches the Boundary line.
_Avoid_: off track, derail

**Starting Line**:
The line where Cars are placed to begin the race. A Car that stops on a
Bridge returns to it.

**Bridge**:
A strip covering the full Track width, chalked with diagonals in both
directions. The Car must cross it within a single Push.

**Hazard**:
A chalk-drawn Track feature with a rule attached (Rock, Lake, Bridge, …).
Hazards are optional — a Track does not need to use every Hazard type.

**Terrain**:
The real-world surface under the Track — pebbles, potholes, bumps, drops
between surfaces at different heights. Found, not drawn; Cars can bounce or
flip on it.

**Surface**:
What a wheel is standing on — concrete, asphalt, tiles, glass. Grip belongs
to the *contact*, not to the Car: it is Surface × tire, read per wheel, every
frame. A single Track crosses Surfaces (tiled balcony onto a concrete path),
so two wheels of one Car can be on two Surfaces at once.
_Avoid_: material, ground, floor (a Surface is a thing with grip, not a colour)

**Rock**:
A solid white triangle obstacle. Touching it with even one wheel sends the
Car back to the closest Checkpoint.

**Lake**:
An ellipse with a diagonal-line hatch pattern. Rolling over it sends the Car
two Checkpoints back.

**Finish Line**:
The line a Car must reach first to win the race.

**Track Building**:
The creative act of drawing a Track and placing Hazards on it — a core part
of the game's fun, not just setup.
